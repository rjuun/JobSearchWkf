## Source

The job description is supplied **inline, in the message you are given**, under `JOB DESCRIPTION:`, followed by `CITY:` and — when available — the candidate's `CANDIDATE VALUES & MOTIVES:` summary. That text is the complete and only source for this step.

Do not attempt to open, fetch, or look up a file, a workbook, a SharePoint list, or any other external location — you have no access to one. The city preference lists in §B are stated in this note in full, for the same reason.

---

## A. Values and Motives

A **Values and Motives Misalignment** occurs when signals in the JD conflict with the candidate's core values, as given to you inline under `CANDIDATE VALUES & MOTIVES:`. If that section is absent from your message, judge against the red flags below alone and say so in the detail rather than treating the analysis as impossible.

Flag when the JD shows **explicit evidence** of:
**Work culture red flags:**
- Strictly on-site only (no hybrid or remote mention):
	- Culture: On-site only
- Language suggesting burnout culture ("always on," "high-pressure," "24/7 availability")
	- Culture: Burnout signals
- Explicit KPI-only or individual performance framework with no collaborative language
	- Culture: Non-cooperative

**Ethical/purpose red flags:**
- Industry or products conflicting with ecological, social or ethical values (e.g. weapons, extractive industries, exploitative consumer practices)
	- Ethics: name the specific concern — e.g. *Ethics: Extractive industry*
- Role is purely process/compliance execution with no strategic or meaningful scope
	- Purpose: Transactional only

**Leadership red flags:**
- Explicit top-down, non-participatory structures ("reports to and executes directives of")
	- Leadership: Top-down

**Important:** Most misalignments at this stage are tentative. The JD is a marketing document. Flag, proceed, and validate during company research or interview stages. A misalignment flag does **not** stop the process — it is a conscious awareness marker.

## B. Cities Preferences

A **City Misalignment** occurs when the required work location falls outside the candidate's accepted cities. The preference ranking is stored in the `offices` table; the lists below are the same data, restated here because you are not sent that table. The city for this lead is supplied inline under `CITY:`.

- **Primary cities** (Vienna, Amsterdam, Copenhagen, London, Milan) — no misalignment flag.
- **Secondary cities** (Barcelona, Madrid, Hamburg, Berlin, Stuttgart, München, Salzburg, Zurich, Stockholm, Dublin) — flag, naming the city, as *Secondary — acceptable*.
- **Last Option** (Budapest, Toronto, São Paulo) — flag, naming the city, as *Last Option — proceed with caution*.
- **Not listed** — flag, naming the city, as *Not in scope*.

Note: hybrid/remote roles with occasional travel to a non-primary city are not automatic misalignments — apply judgment on degree of physical presence required.

## C. Seniority Mismatch

A **Seniority Misalignment** occurs when the role's target experience level is 
materially different from the candidate's profile (15+ years, senior leadership).

**Overqualified — detection signals:**
- Explicit years of experience stated materially below 15 
  (e.g., "3–5 years", "4–7 years")
- Role descriptors signalling early/mid-career: "first leadership role", 
  "leadership development programme", "Associate" (non-senior level)
- Salary range disclosed well below senior market level
- Reporting structure: role reports to a position typically held by 
  someone at the candidate's own level
→ Flag as `Seniority: Overqualified`, naming the specific signal in the detail — e.g. *"role targets 4–7 years / first leadership role"*

**Underqualified — detection signals:**
- Role requires deep domain-specific experience the candidate 
  cannot credibly demonstrate (e.g., "10+ years in CIB operations", 
  "15+ years in manufacturing PMO")
- Mandatory industry certifications outside profile scope
→ Flag as `Seniority: Underqualified`, naming the specific signal in the detail

**Important:** A Seniority Misalignment does **not** stop the process — it is 
a conscious awareness marker. Proceed if the role is otherwise compelling, 
but validate fit explicitly during the interview stage.
If the underqualification is severe and the hiring team is likely to screen 
out the application at CV level, consider treating it as a functional Roadblock.

## D. Persistence

This step emits a single `emit_misalignments` tool call. The app validates it (`lib/llm/schemas.ts` →
`B4.zod`) and writes the array to `job_leads.misalignments` (`lib/pipeline/screening.ts`). No standalone
output, export or table is produced — do not generate one.

One entry per misalignment, each carrying:

- **`dimension`** — the category: `City`, `Culture`, `Ethics`, `Purpose`, `Leadership`, `Seniority`.
- **`detail`** — the specific finding in prose, naming the city, signal or concern. Keep the dimension
  out of the detail; it is already its own field.
- **`severity`** *(optional)* — where the strength of the flag is itself worth recording.

**When no misalignments are found, emit an empty array.** Do not emit an entry whose detail is `None` —
that would record a misalignment called "None" on the lead.

**A misalignment never stops the process.** It parks the lead in the scoring queue for a human
decision (`gateStatusFor`) and is otherwise an awareness marker, exactly as §A and §C say twice above.
Nothing here abandons a lead.

*Historic (superseded): this step previously wrote a formatted text block into the "Misalignment"
column of the Job Leads SharePoint list, one `Dimension: detail` line per entry, and the literal `None`
when there were none — back when it ran through the Microsoft 365 connector.*
