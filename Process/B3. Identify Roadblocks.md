## Source

The job description is supplied **inline, in the message you are given**, under `JOB DESCRIPTION:`, followed where available by the requirements B2 extracted, under `REQUIREMENTS:`. That text is the complete and only source for the *role* side of this step.

The *candidate* side is *this note*. The language levels in §A, the Missing Technical Requirements in §B, the Missing Certifications in §C and the industry lists in §E are stated here in full, deliberately — they are the profile facts this step judges against, and you are not sent the profile tables themselves.

Do not attempt to open, fetch, or look up a file, a workbook, a SharePoint list, or any other external location — you have no access to one, and nothing outside this note and the supplied text is needed.

## A. Obligatory Language Requirements

A **Language Roadblock** occurs when a job description requires a level of language proficiency that I **do not currently possess**, per the levels stated below (the `languages` table records the same certificates).
### 1. German Language Requirements
Although I hold a **C1 Zertifikat**, my practical German level is **Business Conversational**. This means:
- I can operate professionally in German
- but I may struggle with **complex communication**, **nuanced leadership language**, or **high‑stakes stakeholder interactions**

Therefore:
- If a job description requires **native‑level**, **near‑native**, **excellent written German**, or **leadership‑grade German**, → **Flag this as a Language Roadblock.**

### 2. Spanish Language Requirements
Although I hold only a **B2 Certification**, my practical Spanish level is **Business-level Spanish**. For Portuguese speakers, it is quite easy to participate in Business Meetings without a problem. This means:
- I can fully operate professionally in Spanish

### 3. Other Languages
If a job description requires a language other than the ones named above (English, German, Spanish, Portuguese), → **Flag this as a Language Roadblock.**

## B. Obligatory Technical Requirements

A **Technical Roadblock** occurs when a job description requires a specific **software or platform** the candidate does not have. The authoritative list of what the candidate *does* have is the `skills_master` table, which you are not sent; judge instead against the **List of Missing Technical Requirements** below, which is maintained here for exactly that reason.

When you identify such a missing requirement:
1. Flag it explicitly as a **Technical Roadblock** and name the software/platform.
2. Compare the requirement against the following list of **Missing Technical Requirements** (skills I do _not_ possess). Any strict requirement for these must always be treated as a Roadblock.

### List of Missing Technical Requirements
1. SAP
2. Salesforce

## D. Geographic Delivery Scope

A **Geographic Scope Roadblock** occurs when a role requires mandatory delivery, travel or territorial responsibility in regions outside acceptable scope — regardless of where the office is located.

Use dimension `Geographic`, and name the region and the obligation in the detail — e.g. *"Middle East — mandatory delivery and travel scope"*.


## E. Industry-Specific Experience

An **Industry Roadblock** occurs when a job description mandates deep, domain-specific experience in an industry the candidate has not worked in, and where that experience is a core hard requirement — not a preference.

**Detection signals:**
- Explicit language: "several years of experience in the [X] industry", "extensive background in [sector]", "deep knowledge of [industry-specific concepts]" in the requirements section (not the preferred qualifications)
- The role's core tasks require industry knowledge that is non-transferable without direct sector experience (e.g., OEM quotation cost structures in automotive, GxP manufacturing compliance in pharmaceuticals)
- The company is a sector specialist where all roles presuppose industry fluency (e.g., automotive Tier-1 supplier, defence contractor, upstream oil & gas)

**Industries NOT in the candidate's profile (hard industry roadblocks when explicitly required):**
1. Automotive (OEM / Tier-1 supplier manufacturing)
2. Defence / Aerospace
3. Mining / Resources extraction
4. Oil & Gas (upstream / midstream)
5. Pharmaceuticals / Life Sciences (GxP manufacturing context only)


> [!NOTE] Industries within profile — not roadblocks
> The following Industries are within the candidate's profile:
> - Financial Services,
> - Banking,
> - Insurance,
> - SaaS / Tech,
> - Consulting,
> - FMCG,
> - Industrial / Manufacturing (general finance roles), and
> - Shared Services
>
> Finance or transformation roles in these sectors are **not** Industry Roadblocks even if industry experience is preferred.


> [!WARNING] **ACCURACY IMPROVEMENT TIP** — for the human review loop, not this call
> An industry explicitly required, absent from both lists above, and outside the candidate's
> experience is a candidate for the roadblock list in this note. Still flag it on this lead; the list
> is what stops it being re-judged from scratch next time.

Use dimension `Industry`, and name the sector and why the experience is non-transferable — e.g.
*"Automotive — mandatory OEM supplier experience"*, or *"Defence — active security clearance required"*.

## F. Persistence

This step emits a single `emit_roadblocks` tool call. The app validates it (`lib/llm/schemas.ts` →
`B3.zod`) and writes the array to `job_leads.roadblocks` (`lib/pipeline/screening.ts`). No standalone
output, export or table is produced — do not generate one.

One entry per roadblock, each carrying:

- **`dimension`** — exactly one of `Language`, `Technical`, `Certification`, `Geographic`, `Industry`.
  These five are the schema's enum; the section headings above map onto them one for one.
- **`detail`** — the specific finding in prose. *"German at native level required"*, *"SAP"*,
  *"CPA Canada"*. Keep the dimension out of the detail; it is already its own field.
- **`requirementOrder`** *(optional)* — when a roadblock maps cleanly onto exactly one of the numbered
  requirements you were given, its number. A native-German demand maps onto a German-language
  requirement; an industry roadblock implied across the whole posting maps onto none. **Do not force a
  mapping** — the app resolves this number to a real requirement row, and a wrong number attaches the
  roadblock to the wrong requirement.

**When no roadblocks are found, emit an empty array.** Do not emit an entry whose detail is `None` —
that would record a roadblock called "None" on the lead. "Analysed, nothing found" and "not yet
analysed" are distinguished by whether this step has run, not by a sentinel value.

Roadblocks do not kill a lead. They park it in the scoring queue for a human decision
(`gateStatusFor`); nothing here abandons a lead automatically.

*Historic (superseded): this step previously wrote a formatted text block into the "Roadblocks" column
of the Job Leads SharePoint list, one `Dimension: detail` line per roadblock, and the literal `None`
when there were none — back when it ran through the Microsoft 365 connector.*
