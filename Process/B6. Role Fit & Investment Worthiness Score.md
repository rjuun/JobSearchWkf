## Purpose

Evaluate how well a Job Lead aligns with the candidate’s actual profile, experience level, and strengths.
This step replaces the previous [[B6. Run Initial ATS Matching Rating]] ATS-heavy scoring (retired version) and produces a more balanced assessment of whether the role is worth investing tailoring time in.

It uses the **Master Bullet Bank** (the `bullet_bank` table) as the primary reference and generates a structured **Role Fit Score** that considers both requirement coverage and broader strategic fit.

---

## A. Inputs

Everything this step needs is **supplied inline, in the message you are given**:

1. **`CANDIDATE EVIDENCE (cite by exact ref code):`** — the Master Bullet Bank in full, plus the candidate's education and languages (the three sources §B.1.2 names). One line per item, formatted `[<ref>] (<kind>) <text>`. **This listing is the complete universe of citable evidence**; if something is not in it, it does not exist for this step.
2. **`CANDIDATE FACTS`** (present only when the owner has filled them in at Identity) — fixed eligibility facts: citizenship/work authorization, relocation, travel. Not evidence to cite by ref code — weigh these for a requirement that's really an eligibility check ("must have permission to work in the EU", "willing to relocate") rather than a skill.
3. **`JOB DESCRIPTION:`** — the posting text.
4. **`REQUIREMENTS:`** — this lead's requirements as B2 extracted them, numbered, each prefixed with its group.

Do not attempt to open, fetch, or look up a file, a workbook, a SharePoint list, or any other external location — you have no access to one, and nothing outside the supplied text is needed.

Do **not** use a tailored CV at this stage. Scoring is based on the supplied evidence listing to ensure consistency and reduce tailoring bias — which is precisely why the listing is the Master Bullet Bank rather than any lead-specific document.

The bank is really sent now (CI · *B6 Never Receives the Master Bullet Bank*).

## B. Scoring Dimensions

Each Job Lead is scored across five dimensions. These dimensions are designed to answer:

> “Is this role a good strategic fit, and is it worth investing time to tailor for?”

| Dimension                        | Weight | What it evaluates                                      | Source                          | Scoring Guidance |
|----------------------------------|--------|--------------------------------------------------------|----------------------------------|------------------|
| **Relevance to the Role**        | 35%    | Overall alignment with the nature, scope, and environment of the role | Master Bullet Bank + qualitative judgment | Highest weight. Consider role type, industry exposure, ways of working, and strategic scope. |
| **Seniority & Strategic Level**  | 20%    | Level of responsibility, exposure to leadership, and strategic scope | Master Bullet Bank              | Evaluate Board/CEO exposure, team leadership, cross-functional influence, and decision-making authority. |
| **Impact & Results**             | 20%    | Strength and clarity of outcomes and achievements      | Master Bullet Bank              | Favour bullets with clear, preferably quantified results. |
| **Requirement Alignment**        | 15%    | How well the profile covers the specific JD requirements | Per-Requirement Scoring (from B5 logic) | Derived from the weighted average of Core / Important / Nice-to-Have requirements. |
| **ATS Compatibility**            | 10%    | Closeness of language between JD and Master Bullet Bank | Qualitative assessment          | Assess how much rewording would be needed. Lower weight because it can often be improved during tailoring. |

**Overall Role Fit Score** = Weighted average of the five dimensions (rounded to 1 decimal place).


---

### B.1 Per-Requirement Scoring Rules

#### 1 Match Strength Scale

| Match Strength | Meaning                                                                           | Score range |
| -------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------- |
| Excellent      | Bullet Bank addresses this requirement fully and explicitly, with strong keywords | 9–10        |
| Very Strong    | Bullet Bank addresses it clearly; minor phrasing or depth gap                     | 7–8         |
| Good           | Bullet Bank covers it partially; evidence is present but not prominent            | 5–6         |
| Weak           | Bullet Bank has tangential or indirect evidence only                              | 2–4         |
| No Match       | No relevant evidence in the Bullet Bank                                           | 0–1         |

Match Strength and Score must be **consistent** — do not assign "Excellent" with a score of 6 or "Weak" with a score of 8.

#### 2 Per-Requirement Score (0–10)

For each requirement:
- Map it to the strongest available evidence in the supplied listing, and **cite every ref code that genuinely supports it** in `evidenceRefs` — several where several apply, none where none do. The relationship is deliberately many-to-many: one requirement is routinely carried by several bullets across several positions, and that is what the Requirement→Evidence Map exists to show.
- Education (`EDU-n`) and language (`LANG-n`) items are in the same listing and are cited the same way — use them for academic and language requirements.
- **Cite only codes that appear in that listing. Never invent one.** A fabricated code is dropped before it is stored, so inventing one loses the citation rather than strengthening it.
- Put the reason the evidence carries the requirement in `evidenceNote` — one sentence, in place of quoting the bullet back.
- Score honestly — never inflate scores.
- State what the evidence covers in `keyStrengths`, and what it does not in `gaps`.
- If no relevant evidence exists, set `No Match`, leave `evidenceRefs` empty, and state in `gaps` what is missing. On `No Match` the gap is mandatory prose — never leave it blank.

**ATS Keyword Check**: Verify whether the JD terminology appears in the supplied evidence. Significant mismatches should be noted in `gaps`, even where the underlying experience exists.

#### 3 Final Per-Requirement Alignment Score (0–10)

The weighted average of all per-requirement scores:

| Requirement Group | Weight |
| ----------------- | ------ |
| Core              | 3      |
| Important         | 2      |
| Nice-to-Have      | 1      |

`Requirement Alignment Score = Σ(score × weight) ÷ Σ(weight)`

> **You do not compute this, and you must not try.** The app calculates Requirement Alignment, the Overall Role Fit Score and the recommendation deterministically from the numbers you return (`lib/scoring.ts`), so the same judgments always produce the same score. It is documented here because it tells you what your per-requirement scores are *for*: a Core requirement moves the outcome three times as much as a Nice-to-Have.

## C. Execution Sequence

One lead per call — there is no batch loop here; the app runs leads seconds apart and batches them itself.

1. Read the supplied evidence listing, the JD and the numbered requirements.
2. Score **every** requirement individually, following **B.1**, and set each one's `order` to its number in the supplied list. Return one judgment per requirement given — an answer that omits any of them is re-asked and then discarded in full, so a partial reply scores the lead on nothing rather than on less.
3. Assess the four judged dimensions — Relevance, Seniority, Impact, ATS — giving particular attention to **Relevance to the Role**.
4. Write the qualitative `summary`: key strengths of the fit, main gaps or concerns.
5. Emit the single tool call described in Section D. Do not compute the overall score or the recommendation.

## D. Persistence

This step emits a single `emit_role_fit` tool call. The app validates it (`lib/llm/schemas.ts` →
`B6.zod`) and writes to three places (`lib/pipeline/screening.ts`):

| What you return | Where it is written |
| --- | --- |
| `relevance`, `seniority`, `impact`, `ats` | `job_leads.score_*` — the four judged dimensions |
| per-requirement `score`, `matchStrength` | `job_requirements.initial_score`, `initial_match_strength` |
| per-requirement `keyStrengths`, `gaps` | `job_requirements.initial_key_strengths`, `initial_missing_weak` |
| per-requirement `evidenceRefs`, `evidenceNote` | `requirement_evidence` — one row per cited ref, replaced wholesale on each re-score |
| `summary` | carried in the run record |

No standalone output, export or table is produced — do not generate one.

**Requirement Alignment, the Overall Role Fit Score, the recommendation and the bank version are
computed by the app, never by you.** The overall is the weighted average of the five dimensions
(§B), the recommendation is derived from it, and `bullet_bank_version` records the version of the
evidence you were actually sent.

Three things that decide whether this step's output is usable:

- **Every requirement gets a judgment, and `order` identifies it.** The app matches your judgments
  back to rows by that number, counts how many rows a judgment actually reached, and re-asks if any
  were missed. If the re-asks still come up short the whole run fails and nothing is written — a
  partial answer is never partially kept. Do not stop early and do not merge two requirements into
  one entry.
- **Filler is worse than an honest low score.** Never emit placeholder text — the literal word
  `placeholder`, `TBD`, `...`, or a note to yourself about redoing the answer — in `summary`,
  `evidenceRefs`, or any other field. Those values are structurally valid, so nothing downstream
  rejects them; they are simply stored as if they were your judgment. If the honest answer is "no
  evidence supports this", that is `No Match` with empty `evidenceRefs` and a real sentence in `gaps`.
- **Blank is not the same as absent.** Where you genuinely have nothing to say for `keyStrengths` or
  `gaps`, return an empty string — it is normalized to null on the way in, and a later reader can
  then tell "B6 had nothing to add" from "B6 never ran".
- **Citations are verified, not trusted.** Every `evidenceRefs` code is checked against the listing
  you were given; unknown codes are counted, warned about, and dropped. The score stands, but the
  evidence lane for that requirement is left empty.

## E. Notes & Quality Rules

- Be honest and conservative. Do not inflate scores to justify pursuing a role.
- "Relevance to the Role" should carry the highest weight and should reflect strategic and contextual fit, not just requirement coverage.
- ATS Compatibility has the lowest weight because language gaps can often be addressed during tailoring.
- The bank version is recorded automatically from the evidence you were sent — you do not state it.
- Flag any requirement that was scored on weak or indirect evidence, in its `gaps`.
