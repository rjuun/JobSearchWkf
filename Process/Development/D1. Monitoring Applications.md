## A. What this phase is for

Once a CV goes out, the question stops being "is this worth my time?" and becomes "what's
outstanding?". Before this existed, that answer lived in memory and in the mailbox — there was no
record of when a confirmation arrived, whether an interview got scheduled, or which applications
quietly died.

The phase covers three moments and one reference view:

1. the application going out,
2. an interview being scheduled,
3. the application being stopped,
4. and, afterwards, a body of finished cases to compare a new lead against.

## B. The capture gesture

**Outcomes are tracked by dragging the email, not by typing.** The email is the record; re-keying
its date and subject into a form is the thing worth removing.

Working practice: confirmations and declines are first filed into two named Outlook folders —
**Bewerbungen** and **Absagen** — and dragged from there onto the matching row in RoleProof. The app
doesn't care which folder a file came from; the on-screen copy names them because that's the gesture
actually being made.

Dragging a message out of **Outlook Classic** hands the browser a real `.msg` file (CDFV2 compound
binary). RoleProof stores that copy under `applications/{leadId}/` and records a link to **its own
copy** — not a live mailbox deep-link. This is a decision, not a shortfall: an
`outlook.office.com/mail/deeplink/…` URL only exists because Outlook generates it from the live
mailbox, and a dragged file cannot be turned into one after the fact. "Live link" and "the file
you're already dragging" were never the same option.

A drop that carries no file and no link (some clients hand over nothing across a window boundary)
opens a short manual form pre-filled with today instead. The gesture works either way.

## C. The three moments

| Moment | Gesture | Recorded |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Application sent** | drop the confirmation email on the lead's board row or its workspace panel | `applications.status = 'response_pending'`, send date, link to the archived confirmation; the lead moves to `applied` |
| **Interview scheduled** | drop the invite on the row in **Applications** | `status = 'interview'`, setup date, link to the archived invite |
| **Interview date** | typed by hand | `interviewAt` — the one fact no email carries, because it's in the future |
| **Stopped** | drop the decline on the row | `status = 'screened_out'`, decline date, link to the archived decline; the row moves to the Archive |

A portal application with no confirmation email at all is the one case with no email to drag: the
same control accepts a two-step manual confirm instead. That is a fallback for a real gap, not an
equal alternative to dropping.

The lead's own `job_leads.status` stays at `applied` throughout. These are moments *inside* being
applied, not new stages of the lead.

## D. Surfaces

- **Applications** (`/roleproof/applications`) — everything still in play, response-pending and
  interview-scheduled in one list. Two states of the same object; splitting them would mean
  checking two places to answer one question. A row with no word for **7+ days** wears a `stale`
  badge.
- **Archive** (`/roleproof/archive`) — stopped applications only, for pattern-matching when a new
  lead resembles one that didn't go anywhere. Every row links back into the lead's full detail
  (scores, must-haves, the CV that was sent), which is the actual point of the view.

Leads dropped at the *screening* gate (`roadblocked` / `misaligned`) do **not** appear in the
Archive. They never had an application to stop. That data stays on record for a future
throughput/pattern-mining view.

## E. Deliberately not part of this

- No Microsoft Graph integration, no OAuth, no live mailbox links (§B).
- No `.msg` / `.eml` parsing — subject and date are not auto-extracted; the manual form covers it.
- No outbound send. The decline reply-assist offers copy-to-clipboard and a `mailto:` handoff to
  the user's own mail client; RoleProof has no SMTP or Graph send path.
