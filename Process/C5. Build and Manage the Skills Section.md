## Purpose

Create and maintain the dedicated **Skills section** at the top of the CV (positioned between the Profile and Professional Experience). This section serves as the single source of truth for the candidate’s relevant skills for a specific Job Lead.

This step aims to:

- Provide a clear, well-organized, and ATS-friendly overview of the candidate’s skills.
- Ensure consistency between the skills mentioned in the Professional Experience section and the top Skills List.
- Improve both human readability and ATS performance by maintaining one authoritative Skills section.

---

## A. Execution Sequence

1. Retrieve **`cv_bullet_skills`** from every Keep-gated row produced in [[C2. Map JD Requirements to Supporting Evidence]] and written by [[C4. Transform Evidence into CV Bullets]] — the skills each tailored bullet actually displays, whether [[C4. Transform Evidence into CV Bullets]] presented them as a bracketed tag or as selective bold inline. **Only Keep-gated rows**: a requirement with no matched, kept evidence behind it has nothing to claim, so its skills do not print.
2. **Prioritise** them by the matched requirement's rank — Core first, then Important, then Nice-to-Have — keeping each skill once, at its best rank, and cutting to what the section can hold (see Prioritization below). This step decides *which* skills print, and nothing else.
3. **Consolidate** the near-duplicates per §B.5 — one capability, one entry, with the entry that prints naming the skills it replaces. This is the only step that sees the whole set, so it is the only step that can see a duplicate at all.
4. **Categorise** what survived into 3–5 logical groups per §B.1 below. Name the categories from the skills themselves and this role's focus — they are capability areas, never the requirement ranks, and never a generic bucket like "Other".
5. Review the Skills section for clarity, structure, and completeness.
6. Produce the final Skills section ready for use in **C7. Compile Complete CV Document**.

## B. Skills Section Construction Rules

### 1. Structure and Categorization

The Skills section should be organized into **logical categories** rather than presented as one long flat list. Categories should reflect the main capability areas relevant to the Job Lead.

**Recommended approach:**

- Group skills into 3–5 high-level categories.
- Place the most relevant categories (aligned with Core requirements) at the top.
- Keep each category concise (ideally 4–8 skills per category).

**Example structure:**

- Transformation & Process Excellence
- Leadership & Stakeholder Management
- Governance, Risk & Compliance
- Strategic Execution & Change

### 2. Consistency Rule (Mandatory)

Every value in **`cv_bullet_skills`** on a Keep row **must** appear in the top Skills List. This is what makes the rule self-bounding: the source is the finite set of skills the JD's own requirements ask for, restricted to the requirements that actually have matched evidence — not an open-ended tag vocabulary.

**Rationale:**

- The top Skills List acts as the **single source of truth**.
- This ensures the CV remains consistent and professional.
- It prevents situations where a skill is referenced in the experience section but is missing from the main Skills overview. Sourcing from `cv_bullet_skills` is what makes this literally true: those are exactly the skills the bullets below display, so the two sections cannot disagree.

> [!Note]
> The old form of this rule — every **My Skills** value must print, unconditionally — was rationalised the same way but could not deliver it, because nothing in the rendered experience section ever referenced a My Skills value. Bullets print as plain text; their skills are the bracketed or bolded `Requirement Skills`. The rule was guarding a correspondence that did not exist, against a vocabulary that had no bound.

### 3. Prioritization

- Prioritize skills that were associated with **Core** and **Important** requirements during C4.
- Skills linked only to Nice-to-Have requirements can be included if space allows, but they are secondary.

This is implemented literally: the rank of the requirement each Keep row answers decides the heading its skills print under, and a skill claimed by more than one rank prints once, under the highest.

Rank determines the **order skills are taken in**, and therefore what falls off when the section is full: Core first, then Important, then Nice-to-Have, then any skill whose requirement carries no rank. A skill claimed by several requirements is kept once, at its best rank.

The section holds up to §B.1's envelope — 5 categories × 8 skills. Everything beyond that is shed from the bottom of the priority order, which is exactly the owner's *"as the number of declared skills allows"*.

> [!Note]
> **That envelope stopped binding on 2026-08-25 and the size target moved to §B.5.** Raising the register ([[C4 Writes CV-Grade Skill Tags]]) and cutting the bullet budget ([[C3 Selects the CV Evidence Set]]) between them brought the three measured leads to 26 / 31 / 30 skills — under the cap of 40 and above the 16–20 benchmark. Lowering the cap would close that gap by **shedding real capabilities**, which is the one way of reaching the number that costs the CV something. Merging closes it by carrying every claim forward under fewer names.

> [!Note]
> **Prioritisation decides which skills print; it is not the category scheme.** Rank orders the skills and decides what falls off when the section is full — it never becomes a heading. Headings come from §B.1: 3–5 capability areas named over the surfaced set.
>
> *Superseded (2026-08-24):* between 2026-08-23 and 2026-08-24 this step printed the rank names themselves as headings — "Core Competencies / Supporting Expertise / Additional Skills". That implemented §B.3 and left §B.1 unbuilt, and since most leads land nearly everything under Core it did not deliver the vertical readability §B.1 exists for. ### 4. Formatting Guidelines

**What never appears in the Skills section:**

- **Languages.** The CV has a dedicated **Languages** section, filled from the profile's own language records. A language is never a Skills entry — not as "German (C1)", not as "Business-Fluent English", not folded into a compound entry such as "Bilingual Communication". Stating the same fact twice in two shapes wastes a Skills slot and reads as padding. This is unconditional: it holds even when the Job Description lists language fluency as a Core requirement, because the requirement is answered by the Languages section, not by repeating it here.

- Use clear category headings.
- Keep skill names concise and consistent in style.
- Avoid overly long lists within each category.
- Ensure the section remains easy to scan for both human readers and ATS systems.

### 5. Consolidation of Near-Duplicates (Mandatory)

`cv_bullet_skills` is written one bullet at a time by [[C4. Transform Evidence into CV Bullets]], which is called per evidence ref and never sees the assembled set. Each bullet earns its own anchor, so **one capability arrives several times, separated only by its qualifier**. Measured across three leads on 2026-08-25: *Senior Stakeholder Negotiation · Senior Stakeholder Coordination · Senior Stakeholder Management (Multi-Entity) · (Multi-Country) · (Board & Regulator) · Multi-Country Stakeholder Coordination* — six entries, one capability. C5 is the only step with whole-set vision, so this is treated here or nowhere.

**The rule.** Skills that name one capability print as **one entry**. The entry that prints must **declare what it replaces** — `mergedFrom` on `emit_skill_groups` — and the skills it replaces do not print anywhere else. Declaring is what separates a merge from an invention: a name that quietly appears without saying which skills it stands for is dropped, because nothing then ties it to evidence the bullets carry.

**What is one capability, and what only looks like one.** The same capability exercised at different scope, in different entities or before different audiences is one entry. Two different capabilities that happen to share a word are two entries — *Transfer Pricing (OECD)* and *Cost Allocation* are not duplicates, and neither are *Board-Level Advisory* and *Board Reporting*. When in doubt, leave them apart: printing a real capability twice costs a line, and merging two real capabilities loses one.

**The qualifier judgement.** Those parentheticals are precision [[C4. Transform Evidence into CV Bullets]] added on purpose, and a merge must not quietly spend them. A merged entry has to stay **wider than every skill it replaces**: keep the qualifier that still holds, or combine them where they combine — *Senior Stakeholder Management (Board, Regulator & Multi-Country)*. Which anchor survives is a judgement over this lead; **surviving with none of them is not**. Collapsing a qualified family to the bare capability is atomisation, it drops what the evidence earned, and it is refused downstream.

**Size follows from this, and from nothing else.** The section should read at **16–20 entries**. The way down to that number is merging — never dropping. A capability with nothing to merge into keeps its entry even if the section stays long, and a long section is the honest signal that the bullets behind it are spread wide.

## C. Output

**Primary Output:** A structured **Skills section** ready to be inserted into the CV during **C7. Compile Complete CV Document**.

The output should include:

- Category headings
- Skills listed under each category
- Confirmation that the Consistency Rule has been applied

---

## D. Quality & Consistency Rules

- The top Skills List is the **master reference** for skills on the CV.
- Any skill referenced in the Professional Experience section must be present in the Skills List.
- Do not duplicate skills across categories — and do not print one capability twice **within** a category either. The rule said only "across" until 2026-08-25, which is how six spellings of senior stakeholder work printed side by side in one line; §B.5 is what it now means.
- Maintain visual consistency in formatting (same style for all categories and skills).
- After building the Skills List, perform a final cross-check with the bullets from C4 to ensure alignment.
- The mandatory inclusions are every `cv_bullet_skills` value carried by a Keep row. There is no separate top-up pass: the Keep set already defines the whole list, and a skill with no kept evidence behind it has no claim on the section.
- `tbl_Skills_Master`, `tbl_STAR_Competences` and `tbl_STAR_Attributes` are still the recognised vocabulary, but they are consumed one step earlier — at C2, as the closed list **My Skills** is selected from. All three count (epic Q3, answered 2026-08-23): a Job Description does not distinguish a skill from a competence from an attribute, even though the profile tables do.

### Notes

- Note: C5 does not pull skills directly from B5, and its **categories have nothing to do with B5's Areas of Expertise or JD Groups** — those describe the *role*; these describe the *skills that surfaced for it*. C5 organizes the `cv_bullet_skills` written onto each Keep row by C4 — the tag that bullet actually carries. `requirement_skills` sits beside it, unmodified since C2, holding what the JD asked for. This is what ensures strong consistency between the Professional Experience section and the top Skills section: both print the same values.
- This step (C5) focuses exclusively on the **top Skills section** of the CV. The logic for associating skills _inside_ individual bullets is handled in **C4**.
- By making the top Skills List the single source of truth, we reduce the need to force skills into every bullet and improve overall CV clarity and professionalism.
- C5 should be executed after C4 (once the bullets and their associated skills are defined).
