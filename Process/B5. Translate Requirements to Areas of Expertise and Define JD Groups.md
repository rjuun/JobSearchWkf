## Context and Origin

This step was designed based on an initial exercise with 110 Job Descriptions.

The 110 positions were categorised into 6 JD Groups:
1. Strategy & Corporate Development
2. Chief of Staff & Executive Office
3. Operations & Shared Services
4. Controlling / FP&A / Finance
5. Transformation & Project Management
6. Procurement / Outsourcing / ESG

From this exercise, 12 Areas of Expertise were initially identified:
A. Strategic Planning | Corporate Development
B. Corporate Governance
C. Operations | Back-Office | Shared Services
D. Controlling
E. Financial Planning and Analysis
F. Project Management
G. Process Management
H. Procurement | Outsourcing
I. ESG | Sustainability
J. Change | Transformation Management | Merger Integration
K. Reporting and Analytics (Regulatory and Performance)
L. Banking | Financial Services

An AI Agent then ran a detailed analysis of each of the 110 JD PDF files to:
1. Reassess each AoE rating
2. Comment on possible rating changes vs. the initial superficial rating
3. Provide Key Patterns & CV Tailoring Notes

From this in-depth analysis, 5 additional Areas of Expertise were
identified as significant and added to the framework:
M. Leadership & People Management
N. ERP / SAP / Digital Systems
O. German Language Proficiency
P. Consulting Pedigree / Ex-Consultant Toolkit
Q. Regulatory & Compliance Expertise

The 6 JD Groups were maintained, with the understanding that most JDs belong
to a dominant Primary Group but also exhibit characteristics of a Secondary Group.

Letter codes A–Q were adopted as a consistent shorthand reference; they are now the
keys of `job_leads.skill_ratings`.

## Purpose

For each Job Lead that has passed Steps B.1–B.4, translate the Job Description and the requirements B2 extracted from it into the 17-Areas of Expertise framework, and assign a JD Group classification.

Step outputs feed the lead's `job_leads` row: the 17 Area of Expertise ratings (`skill_ratings`), `jd_group_primary`, `jd_group_secondary`, and Key Patterns & CV Tailoring Notes (`key_patterns`). **Not** `ats_system` — see Section C.

### Rating Scale

| Rating | Label | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Central | Mandatory / Primary Requirement |
| 2 | Contributing | Strong Advantage / Frequently Mentioned |
| 3 | Peripheral | Not required / Not mentioned / Nice-to-have |

> [!WARNING] These labels were renamed — and the rename is the point
> They used to read `Core` / `Important` / `Supporting`, which collided with the
> `Core` / `Important` / `Nice-to-Have` classification that
> [[B2. Extract Requirements from Job Description]] applies to **requirements**.
> Two unrelated scales, overlapping vocabulary, adjacent steps — and the collision
> made it look as though requirement categorisation lived in this step, which it
> never did (CI · *Lead Page as Pipeline Canvas* §2.2c).
>
> **The two scales measure different things.** This one rates how central each of
> the 17 Areas of Expertise is *to the role*, and exists to pick a JD Group. The
> other ranks how mandatory each *extracted requirement* is, using the JD's own
> language, and exists to weight scoring. `Core` / `Important` / `Nice-to-Have`
> now belongs unambiguously to requirements; never use those three words for an
> Area of Expertise rating.
>
> Values and meanings are unchanged — 1, 2 and 3 mean exactly what they meant
> before. Only the words moved.

**Exception — M (Leadership & People Management):**
- `1` = Must lead a team or hold direct reports
- `2` = Influential leadership without formal authority
- `3` = Nice-to-have / not mentioned

### JD Group Decision Rules

| Priority | Group Code | Group Name                          | Primary signal                                |
| -------- | ---------- | ----------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------- |
| 1        | TPM        | Transformation & Project Management | F or J rated 1                                |
| 2        | CSEO       | Chief of Staff & Executive Office   | A + B rated 1 with strong executive proximity |
| 3        | OSS        | Strategy & Corporate Development    | A rated 1                                     |
| 4        | SCD        | Operations & Shared Services        | C rated 1, G rated 2 or 3                     |
| 5        | CFPA       | Controlling, FP&A & Finance         | D or E rated 1                                |
| 6        | POESG      | Procurement, Outsourcing & ESG      | H or I rated 1                                |

When two groups are equally dominant, assign the stronger as Primary and the weaker as Secondary. If no clear secondary group exists, leave `JD Group Secondary` blank.

**Important – JD Group Output Format**
Always emit the full Group Name (e.g. "Chief of Staff & Executive Office"), not the Group Code (e.g. "CSEO").

## B. Source and Execution Sequence

The job description is supplied **inline, in the message you are given**, under the heading `JOB DESCRIPTION:`, followed by the requirements B2 already extracted, under `REQUIREMENTS (extracted at B2):`. Those two are the complete and only source for this step.

Do not attempt to open, fetch, or look up a file, a workbook, a SharePoint list, or any other external location — you have no access to one, and nothing outside the supplied text is needed.

1. Read the supplied JD and the extracted requirements
2. Rate all 17 Areas of Expertise against them
3. Write Key Patterns and CV Tailoring Notes (2–4 sentences maximum)
4. Assign Primary and Secondary JD Group
5. Emit the single tool call described in Section D

**One lead per call.** There is no batch loop and no end-of-batch step: the app calls this step once per lead, several leads seconds apart, and does the batching itself.

ATS identification is **not** a step here — see Section C.

> Historic note (superseded): this step previously read a `.md` file from `OneDrive > Obsidian Vault > JobSearch Camunda > Job Descriptions` and looped over a whole batch, back when it ran through the Microsoft 365 connector. The app now passes one lead's JD directly.

**Language note:** JDs written in German are analysed in their original language. Areas of Expertise ratings apply to the role's requirements regardless of the language in which they are expressed.

## C. ATS Identification — removed, now owned by A1

**This step does not identify the ATS.** The section that used to live here was deleted by CI · *Lead Page as Pipeline Canvas* (§2.2a), and nothing replaces it inside this note.

Why: this step receives `JOB DESCRIPTION:\n{jd}` and nothing else — no `sourceUrl`, no Job Post Link, no browsing tool. The deleted instruction ("identify the real ATS by checking the application flow… use the Job Post Link") directed the model to open a link it was never passed and had no means of fetching. Anything it returned was therefore inferred from job-description prose, and ATS identity does not live in prose; it lives in the page chrome — apply form host, embedded iframe, footer branding. Worse, the write path let that inference **overwrite** the deterministic hostname match A1 had already stored, replacing a verified value with an unverifiable one.

ATS is now identified once, at capture, in [[A1. Capture and Store Job Leads]] §B.2 (deterministic hostname match) and §C (inline page extraction by the capturing agent, at zero marginal cost). The canonical ATS name list lives there too — including the Jobvite / UKG Pro / Umantis entries this note used to carry. A re-check rides on A1's "refresh without re-screening" hook, because re-reading the page is the only thing that can add information.

**Do not emit an ATS value from this step.** `atsSystem` is no longer part of this step's tool schema, and there is no field for it in what this step persists (§D).

## D. Persistence

This step emits a single `emit_skill_mapping` tool call. The app validates it
(`lib/llm/schemas.ts` → `B5.zod`) and writes `skill_ratings` (jsonb, keyed A–Q), `jd_group_primary`,
`jd_group_secondary` and `key_patterns` onto the lead's `job_leads` row
(`lib/pipeline/screening.ts`). No standalone output, export or table is produced — do not generate one.

Two things the tool call must carry, because both were silently dropped for a long time and both are
easy to omit:

- **All 17 dimensions.** Rate every code A–Q, including the ones the JD barely touches. A missing
  dimension is not the same as one rated Peripheral, and `skill_ratings` is read by key.
- **`notes`** — the Key Patterns & CV Tailoring Notes, 2–4 sentences: lead with the dominant CV theme,
  then name 2–3 specific tailoring priorities. This is what `key_patterns` stores.

Use the full **Group Name** for the JD groups (e.g. "Chief of Staff & Executive Office"), not the code
(e.g. "CSEO"). Leave the secondary group empty when no clear one exists rather than forcing a pick.

Confidence is expressed inside `notes`, in prose — there is no separate flag field. Where a rating of
1 rests on ambiguous JD evidence, or where Primary and Secondary are near-equal, say so there.

*Historic (superseded): this step previously produced a per-JD analysis block and an end-of-batch
tab-delimited SharePoint export rendered as an interactive HTML widget with a Copy button, back when
it ran through the Microsoft 365 connector.*
