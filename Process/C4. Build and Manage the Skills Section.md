## Purpose

Create and maintain the dedicated **Skills section** at the top of the CV (positioned between the Profile and Professional Experience). This section serves as the single source of truth for the candidate’s relevant skills for a specific Job Lead.

This step aims to:

- Provide a clear, well-organized, and ATS-friendly overview of the candidate’s skills.
- Ensure consistency between the skills mentioned in the Professional Experience section and the top Skills List.
- Improve both human readability and ATS performance by maintaining one authoritative Skills section.

---

## A. Execution Sequence

1. Retrieve **`cv_bullet_skills`** from every Keep-gated row produced in [[C2. Map JD Requirements to Supporting Evidence]] and written by [[C3. Transform Evidence into CV Bullets]] — the skills each tailored bullet actually displays, whether [[C3. Transform Evidence into CV Bullets]] presented them as a bracketed tag or as selective bold inline. **Only Keep-gated rows**: a requirement with no matched, kept evidence behind it has nothing to claim, so its skills do not print.
2. Order them by the matched requirement's rank — Core first, then Important, then Nice-to-Have (see Prioritization below).
3. Group them under the resulting headings, printing each skill once, under its highest rank.
4. Review the Skills section for clarity, structure, and completeness.
5. Produce the final Skills section ready for use in **C6. Compile Complete CV Document**.

> [!Note]
> **C4 sources from the skills carried by the tailored bullets — `cv_bullet_skills`.** This is the JD's own language, which is what makes the top Skills List and the bullets below it agree word-for-word and is the strongest ATS position available. `cv_bullet_skills` is also the *only* durable record of a bullet's skills: bold-inline integration lives inside the bullet's own text and is never captured separately, so both of C3 §B.5's presentation methods resolve to this one column.
>
> **My Skills is not what the Skills List prints, and never was meant to be.** It is the candidate's own vocabulary — C2's validated selection from `tbl_Skills_Master`, `tbl_STAR_Competences` and `tbl_STAR_Attributes` — and it remains the **traceability chain** from a CV line back to the profile capability it rests on, exactly as C3 §B.5 describes it. It is bullet-level provenance, not a display field.
>
> *Superseded (2026-08-23, [[C4 Skills Selection Produces Unreadable Overflow]]): this section previously read "C4 does not source from the C3 bracketed tags… It sources from My Skills", with a mandatory uncapped rule that every My Skills value on a Keep row must print. At the time My Skills held the evidence node's raw free-text graph tags, so one lead's 64 Keep rows printed 67 names in a single line. Both halves changed: My Skills is now a curated selection made at C2, and it is no longer what this section prints.*

## B. Skills Section Construction Rules

### 1. Structure and Categorization

The Skills section should be organized into **logical categories** rather than presented as one long flat list. Categories should reflect the main capability areas relevant to the Job Lead.

**Recommended approach:**

- Group skills into 3–5 high-level categories.
- Place the most relevant categories (aligned with Core requirements) at the top.
- Keep each category concise (ideally 4–8 skills per category).

**Example structure:**

- Transformation & Process Excellence
- Leadership & Stakeholder Management
- Governance, Risk & Compliance
- Strategic Execution & Change

### 2. Consistency Rule (Mandatory)

Every value in **`cv_bullet_skills`** on a Keep row **must** appear in the top Skills List. This is what makes the rule self-bounding: the source is the finite set of skills the JD's own requirements ask for, restricted to the requirements that actually have matched evidence — not an open-ended tag vocabulary.

**Rationale:**

- The top Skills List acts as the **single source of truth**.
- This ensures the CV remains consistent and professional.
- It prevents situations where a skill is referenced in the experience section but is missing from the main Skills overview. Sourcing from `Requirement Skills` is what makes this literally true: those are exactly the skills the bullets below display, so the two sections cannot disagree.

> [!Note]
> The old form of this rule — every **My Skills** value must print, unconditionally — was rationalised the same way but could not deliver it, because nothing in the rendered experience section ever referenced a My Skills value. Bullets print as plain text; their skills are the bracketed or bolded `Requirement Skills`. The rule was guarding a correspondence that did not exist, against a vocabulary that had no bound.

### 3. Prioritization

- Prioritize skills that were associated with **Core** and **Important** requirements during C3.
- Skills linked only to Nice-to-Have requirements can be included if space allows, but they are secondary.

This is implemented literally: the rank of the requirement each Keep row answers decides the heading its skills print under, and a skill claimed by more than one rank prints once, under the highest.

| Requirement rank | Prints under |
| --- | --- |
| Core | **Core Competencies** |
| Important | **Supporting Expertise** |
| Nice-to-Have (and any unranked) | **Additional Skills** |

Empty categories are omitted rather than printed as bare headings. When the section overruns the size envelope below, **Additional Skills** is what sheds — Core and Important are never truncated, because each one answers a requirement with matched, human-kept evidence behind it, and dropping one to hit a number drops a proven claim. If Core and Important alone overrun the envelope, the section prints long: that is an over-extraction upstream at B2, and it should be visible rather than hidden by the renderer.

> [!Note]
> **These rank-derived headings are a stand-in, and §B.1 above does not ask for them.** §B.1 asks for 3–5 *logical categories* reflecting "the main capability areas relevant to the Job Lead" — thematic groups that make the section readable vertically. Core/Supporting/Additional is a *prioritisation* label wearing a category's clothes; it satisfies §B.3 and leaves §B.1 unimplemented.
>
> *Correction (2026-08-24):* this note previously claimed the thematic taxonomy "does not exist as data yet — tracked separately as ROADMAP P6". Both halves were wrong. ROADMAP P6 covers the `approval_status` rename and per-tenant CV templates, and mentions nothing about skills. And a taxonomy does exist — `jd_groups` (six named capability areas) plus B5's 17-dimension A–Q framework. Tracked as live work on [[C4 Skills Selection Produces Unreadable Overflow]] §2.11.

### 4. Formatting Guidelines

- Use clear category headings.
- Keep skill names concise and consistent in style.
- Avoid overly long lists within each category.
- Ensure the section remains easy to scan for both human readers and ATS systems.

## C. Output

**Primary Output:** A structured **Skills section** ready to be inserted into the CV during **C6. Compile Complete CV Document**.

The output should include:

- Category headings
- Skills listed under each category
- Confirmation that the Consistency Rule has been applied

---

## D. Quality & Consistency Rules

- The top Skills List is the **master reference** for skills on the CV.
- Any skill referenced in the Professional Experience section must be present in the Skills List.
- Do not duplicate skills unnecessarily across categories.
- Maintain visual consistency in formatting (same style for all categories and skills).
- After building the Skills List, perform a final cross-check with the bullets from C3 to ensure alignment.
- The mandatory inclusions are every `cv_bullet_skills` value carried by a Keep row. There is no separate top-up pass: the Keep set already defines the whole list, and a skill with no kept evidence behind it has no claim on the section.
- `tbl_Skills_Master`, `tbl_STAR_Competences` and `tbl_STAR_Attributes` are still the recognised vocabulary, but they are consumed one step earlier — at C2, as the closed list **My Skills** is selected from. All three count (epic Q3, answered 2026-08-23): a Job Description does not distinguish a skill from a competence from an attribute, even though the profile tables do.

### Notes

- Note: C4 does not pull skills directly from B5, and does not run a ranking of its own. It organizes the `cv_bullet_skills` written onto each Keep row by C3 — the tag that bullet actually carries. `requirement_skills` sits beside it, unmodified since C2, holding what the JD asked for. This is what ensures strong consistency between the Professional Experience section and the top Skills section: both print the same values.
- This step (C4) focuses exclusively on the **top Skills section** of the CV. The logic for associating skills _inside_ individual bullets is handled in **C3**.
- By making the top Skills List the single source of truth, we reduce the need to force skills into every bullet and improve overall CV clarity and professionalism.
- C4 should be executed after C3 (once the bullets and their associated skills are defined).
