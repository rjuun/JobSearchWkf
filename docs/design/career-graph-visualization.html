Positions are the big hubs. Stories, responsibilities, competences and attributes fan out around them. The dashed lines are skill evidence — what a story proves. Click through to see the full record.

Mock data source: your Profile Reference Workbook (positions F → A, STARs 1–7, actions, results, responsibilities, competences, attributes, skills master, bullet bank). Hierarchy follows Position→STAR, STAR→Action, STAR→Result and Position→Responsibility (all one-to-many, per your source data). Competences, attributes and skills are recorded at the STAR level in the workbook — not tagged to a specific action or result — so they stay attached to the story rather than being split across actions/results without real data to back that split. A competence or attribute that recurs under the same name across stories (e.g. "Innovativeness" on three separate STARs) is collapsed into one node with a connection to each story, the same pattern skills already use. Competences, attributes and skills also share one node size — CV tailoring treats them as one "skills" language even though the graph keeps them distinct here. CV Bullets (rectangular chips, hidden until toggled in the legend) always link to the CV slot they're placed in; 5 of the 28 bullets in the workbook also cite the specific action/result/responsibility/competence/attribute they were composed from in a free-text note, and those become real edges — e.g. bullet "L2" visibly draws from two competences and one responsibility at once. The other 23 only show their CV-slot link, because no finer source was recorded — nothing here is guessed. Layout is force-directed and settles on load — drag any node to rearrange, scroll to zoom. This is a static design preview — wiring it to the live Career Graph query is a follow-up.
